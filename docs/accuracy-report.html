Short answer

The prototype works from start to finish: a phone video goes in, and a list of furniture with sizes and a total volume comes out. On our test videos, big items like beds and sofas came out at realistic sizes. Low items (coffee tables, benches) came out too short, and the front-to-back depth of items is often underestimated.

We have not yet compared the AI with a tape measure in a real room. The numbers below are our best estimate from test footage. The next step is a proper test with real furniture.

1 · Method

What we tested

We used one real phone video of a study room (5.6 seconds, compressed by WhatsApp to 464 × 832 pixels) and two professional room photos, each turned into a short video by slowly panning and zooming across it. The AI processed all three exactly as it would any phone video.

The real video exposed a bug: heights were measured from the lowest visible part of an item instead of from the floor. It was fixed on 15 October, and the results below are from the fixed version. The fix corrected the desk (0.45 → 0.76 m), the bench (0.33 → 0.48 m) and the office chair (0.23 → 0.67 m).

We don't know the true sizes of the furniture in these photos. So instead of “error versus tape measure”, the table compares the AI's sizes with the normal size range for that kind of furniture. This shows whether the AI is in the right ballpark, but it can't give an exact accuracy figure.

Sizes are length × depth × height in metres. Length is the longer side, depth is front to back.

Small size errors grow when you multiply them into a volume. If each side is 10% too small, the volume is about 27% too small. That is why depth and height errors matter so much for a moving quote.

4 · Known problems

What is wrong right now

  • No real-world accuracy test yet

    High
    Why
    So far we only had stock photos turned into videos. We don't know the true sizes of that furniture.
    Effect
    We can't yet promise the client a tested accuracy figure.

  • Depth (front to back) is underestimated

    High
    Why
    Each frame is measured on its own, and the camera usually sees only the front of an item. When the depth looks too small, the app uses a typical depth for that item type instead.
    Effect
    Sofas and cabinets come out 15–20% too shallow, or get a typical depth instead of a measured one.

  • Low items come out too short when the floor isn't visible

    Medium
    Why
    Height is measured from the floor. When the floor is visible this now works well (fixed 15 Oct). When the floor is out of view, the AI can only measure the visible part of the item.
    Effect
    Coffee tables can still be 25–30% too low if the video never shows the floor.

  • Some items are missed

    Medium
    Why
    In the real video the wardrobe and the door were only partly in view, and the AI didn't recognise them. Large items that never fit fully in the frame are hard to detect.
    Effect
    Missing volume, and no door to calibrate the scale. The customer can add missed items by hand, and recording from further back helps.

  • Overall scale can be off by 10–20%

    Medium
    Why
    Estimating real distance from a normal camera always carries some error. The app corrects this only when it sees an object of standard size: a door (80 in tall in the US) or a dining table.
    Effect
    Without a door in the video, every item in that room can be too big or too small by the same amount.

  • Moving boxes and small belongings aren't counted

    Medium
    Why
    The AI measures furniture and appliances it can see. Clothes, kitchenware and books inside cupboards are invisible to the camera.
    Effect
    A real shipment includes many boxes, so the total can be lower than what the mover will actually ship.

  • Built-in items may be listed

    Medium
    Why
    The AI looks for “cabinet”, “oven” and “dishwasher”, but it can't tell a built-in kitchen cabinet from a movable one.
    Effect
    A kitchen scan may include cabinets that stay in the house. The customer has to remove them.

  • The same item can be counted twice

    Medium
    Why
    The tracker follows items frame by frame. If the camera walks away and comes back to an item later, the AI may treat it as a new one.
    Effect
    Duplicate items in the list. The customer can delete them on the review screen.

  • Some items get the wrong name

    Low
    Why
    Similar items look alike (cabinet and nightstand, bench and ottoman). The app corrects names using size, but a wrong size can lead to a wrong name.
    Effect
    Mostly cosmetic. The volume is what matters for the quote, and the customer can fix names.

  • The demo runs on a laptop

    Medium
    Why
    Cloud GPU hosting (Modal) needs a payment card. For now the AI runs on the developer's laptop behind a free Cloudflare link, using a smaller depth model to fit the laptop's 4 GB GPU.
    Effect
    The link works only while the laptop is on, changes on every restart, and handles one video at a time. Uploads over 100 MB fail. The smaller model is a little less accurate than the cloud version would be.

  • License of the item detector

    Low now · High for launch
    Why
    The item detector (YOLOE by Ultralytics) uses the AGPL license. That's fine for a prototype, but a commercial product needs a paid license or a different detector. The depth model (MoGe-2 by Microsoft) is MIT-licensed and free to use.
    Effect
    Must be solved before a public launch.

Appendix

How the AI measures, in brief

  1. The video is split into 5 frames per second.
  2. YOLOE finds and outlines about 40 kinds of furniture by name, and a tracker follows each item so it's counted once.
  3. For each item, the clearest frames from different angles are chosen.
  4. MoGe-2 turns each chosen frame into 3D points in real metres, and the floor is found and cross-checked between frames.
  5. Points that aren't part of the item are removed (floor between legs, things seen through the item), and a box is fitted around the rest.
  6. The measurements are combined, scaled using a door if one is visible, and checked against normal size ranges. The customer can review and edit everything.
